## What is OKX recurring buy?

OKX recurring buy is a form of dollar-cost averaging, commonly called DCA. You select a fixed purchase amount and a recurring schedule, then OKX attempts to buy the selected cryptocurrency at the chosen intervals.

For example, you might create a plan to spend $30 on BTC every week. Each scheduled order buys the amount of BTC available at that time. The number of coins purchased changes with the market price, but the cash amount stays fixed under the standard recurring-buy plan.

This approach is designed for people who want to spread purchases over time instead of making one large purchase based on a single market price. It does not guarantee a profit, reduce the underlying volatility of crypto, or protect the account from losses.

The main idea is consistency:

- You choose the asset.
- You choose the amount.
- You choose how often to buy.
- OKX processes future orders according to the schedule.
- You can review, pause, resume, edit, or cancel the plan from the account interface where the feature is available.

OKX describes recurring buy as a tool for automated crypto purchases rather than investment advice. Crypto prices can move sharply between scheduled orders, and past performance cannot predict future results.

## What happens if a recurring purchase fails?

A recurring plan can fail for several reasons:

- Insufficient balance.
- An unavailable payment method.
- A card or payment-provider rejection.
- A temporary technical issue.
- Asset suspension or delisting.
- Regional or account restrictions.
- A change to the supported purchase route.

According to OKX’s current recurring-buy help page, the platform can notify users before execution when funds are insufficient. If an order fails, OKX sends an email and displays an action-required message in the plan details. If a plan fails three times in a row, it is automatically paused.

A failed order does not mean the missing purchase is automatically made up later. Check the plan history and account balance before assuming that the schedule is still operating normally.

The recurring-buy bot documentation gives a similar warning: users should ensure sufficient funds before each investment period. If a supported cryptocurrency is suspended or delisted during the process, the bot may stop automatically.

## Is OKX recurring buy suitable for beginners?

The interface is easier than manually placing repeated orders, but “automated” does not mean “risk-free.”

OKX recurring buy may suit you if:

- You want to invest a fixed amount on a schedule.
- You do not want to monitor the market every day.
- You understand that the asset price can fall after each purchase.
- You can maintain the required account balance.
- You are comfortable using an exchange account for scheduled purchases.
- You want to review your allocation periodically instead of constantly changing the plan.

It may be a poor fit if:

- You need guaranteed returns.
- You may need the money in the short term.
- You do not understand the asset being purchased.
- A large price decline would force you to stop at the worst possible time.
- You cannot reliably fund future orders.
- You expect DCA to eliminate losses.

Recurring buy changes the timing process, not the risk of the asset. A schedule can help reduce impulsive timing decisions, but it can also continue buying while a market is falling. That is why the amount and frequency should be small enough to remain manageable if prices move against you.
